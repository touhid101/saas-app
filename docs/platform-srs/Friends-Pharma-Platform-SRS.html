1What the Analysis Found

Ten findings decide the shape of this platform. Six are assets that already exist; four are foundations that do not.

Pharmacy onboarding already exists EXISTS

Public sign-up → approval queue → provisioning of tenant, branch, roles and permissions in one transaction, then a subscription with expiry and grace. This is pharmacy onboarding. It needs extending — documents, verification, storefront profile — not building.

Subscription billing already exists EXISTS

Plans, terms, subscriptions, invoices, payments and a gate that refuses a lapsed tenant. Phase 1 of the roadmap is largely already delivered.

A platform product catalogue already exists EXISTS

GLOBAL_PRODUCT carries name, generic, company, category, strength, unit, pack sizes, barcode, image, suggested MRP/TP and a prescription flag — and branches import from it. The platform-catalogue / pharmacy-catalogue split the marketplace needs is already the model in use.

Customers are records, not accounts MISSING

POS_CUSTOMER_INFO has no password, no credential and no identity column of any kind. A customer today is something a pharmacy writes down. Consumer identity, and everything that depends on it, is entirely new.

There is no geolocation anywhere MISSING

Zero latitude/longitude columns in the whole schema, and no opening hours, delivery radius, delivery fee, minimum order or rating on a branch. "Pharmacies near me" — the first screen of the target app — has no data foundation at all.

There is no order MISSING

A sale is an invoice raised by staff at a till. There is no order table, no acceptance step, no state machine, no rejection, no partial fulfilment. A marketplace order is a different object from a POS sale and must be modelled as one.

Delivery is internal, not a marketplace PARTIALLY EXISTS

POS_DELIVERY_COLLECTION attaches a delivery man to an invoice and records cash collected. There is no courier as an actor, no assignment, no pickup, no ETA, no proof of delivery, no failed-delivery path.

No money moves through the platform MISSING

No commission, wallet or settlement table exists, and there is no payment gateway — no outbound HTTP client of any kind in the backend. Payments are recorded, never captured.

Notification is in-app only PARTIALLY EXISTS

POS_NOTIFICATION plus three scheduled alert jobs. No push, no SMS, no email. A marketplace cannot tell a pharmacy an order arrived.

Prescription control has a hook PARTIALLY EXISTS

GLOBAL_PRODUCT.PRESCRIPTION_FLAG exists and 9 of 29 global products carry it. The data is there; no workflow reads it.

The three additions that make it a marketplace catalogue

  1. Sell online (yes/no) per branch product — a pharmacy must be able to stock something without listing it. Default off.
  2. Maximum order quantity per customer — for scarce or controlled items.
  3. Consumer-grade content — a description, usage and warnings. A consumer page cannot be a name, a strength and a price.

Edge cases that must be designed, not discovered

CaseWhat it demandsStatus
Out of stock after acceptanceThe pharmacy must be able to reduce or remove a line. Partial fulfilment changes the payable amount and therefore the settlement and any refund.PLANNED
Partial fulfilmentRequires an order line to carry both an ordered and a fulfilled quantity, and the money to follow the fulfilled figure.PLANNED
Payment failureA prepaid order must not reach a pharmacy until payment is authorised.PLANNED
Pharmacy offline or closedDiscovery must not offer a pharmacy that cannot accept, and an order must not sit unaccepted overnight. Needs opening hours plus an acceptance timeout.PLANNED
Acceptance timeoutAn unaccepted order should expire rather than hang. Duration is a business decision.BUSINESS DECISION REQUIRED
Delivery failureRetry, return to pharmacy, restock, refund. Each is a decision with a money consequence.PLANNED
Prescription required but not suppliedThe order must be held, not rejected outright, so the customer can supply one.BUSINESS / LEGAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED
Stock reservationBetween acceptance and pickup the stock must be held, or two customers buy the same last box. The batch ledger has no reservation concept today.PLANNED
Recommendation. The order is its own aggregate and produces a POS sales invoice at fulfilment. Overloading POS_TRANSACTION_MST would put an eleven-state machine, acceptance, courier assignment and refunds into the same row that already carries purchases, sales and both kinds of return.

Non-negotiables for a pharmacy app

  • Strength and form appear wherever the product appears — never name and price alone.
  • Prescription-only status is visible in search results, not discovered at checkout.
  • Substitution is never silent. If the exact item cannot be supplied, the customer decides.
  • Money uses the same precision as the backend — never a floating-point double.
  • Bangla and English from the first screen.

10Safety & Compliance

This section names where the platform must be ABLE to enforce a control. It does not state what the law requires. Bangladesh pharmacy regulation — which schedules need a prescription, what a dispensing record must contain, whether a licensed pharmacist must approve a remote sale, and whether medicines may be delivered by a non-pharmacist courier at all — must be confirmed with a qualified adviser before any of this is designed. The recommendation is that every control be configurable rather than hard-coded, so the answer can change without a redeployment.

Provider-independent requirements

  1. A payment is an intent with its own lifecycle, referenced by the order — never a column on it.
  2. Every state change is idempotent and carries the provider reference, so a retried callback cannot double-credit.
  3. Settlement is a ledger, not a report: gross, commission, adjustments, net, payout.
  4. Refunds reverse entries; they never delete them.

PG-10🟠 HighSupplier as participantSuppliers are per-pharmacy records tied to one manufacturer.No cross-platform supplier discovery or wholesale ordering.Phase 4. The manufacturer-scoping rule will need revisiting.
PG-11🟡 MediumStorefront profileNo hours, radius, fee, minimum order or preparation time.Discovery cannot filter, and orders arrive when a pharmacy is shut.Extend the branch profile.
PG-12🟡 MediumRatings and reviewsNone.No trust signal and no ranking input.Phase 2 or 3.
PG-13🟡 MediumVerificationApproval is an operator's judgement with no evidence trail.A regulated trade with no record of why a pharmacy was admitted.Document capture and a verification state.
PG-14🟡 MediumCross-tenant readsIsolation forbids reading another tenant's data — correctly, for staff.Discovery and price comparison require exactly that.Design one narrow, audited public-catalogue read path. Do not weaken the general rule.
PG-15🟡 MediumPlatform financial reportingReports are branch-scoped.No GMV, commission, take rate or platform revenue.New reporting layer above the tenant.
PG-16🟢 LowSupport and disputesNo role, case or ticket.Complaints have nowhere to go.Phase 3+.
PG-17🟢 LowSales discount featureThe table exists but the feature has never worked — 0 rows, PID_BRANCH never set.Promotions are unavailable to the marketplace.Fix the existing defect (baseline GAP-08) before building promotions on it.
Reading the severities. The five critical gaps are not independent problems — they are the five things a marketplace is: an identity to order with, a way to find a shop, an order, money, and a way to tell someone. None can be deferred, and none is a modification of something existing.
The encouraging half is what is not on this list. Costing, stock, batch traceability, tenancy, permissions, subscription and onboarding are all sound and reusable.

Why a modular monolith, not microservices

The existing system is one well-layered Spring Boot application with 297 passing tests, and its hardest correctness properties — costing, tenancy isolation, batch integrity — depend on transactions that would span service boundaries. Receiving stock writes a batch, a movement and an invoice atomically; splitting that costs a distributed transaction and buys nothing at this volume.

The recommendation is modules with enforced boundaries inside one deployable, talking to the core through interfaces — extractable later if scale ever justifies it.

The three new integrations change the system's character. The backend today has no outbound HTTP client at all. A payment provider, a push service and an SMS gateway introduce the first external dependencies in its life — and with them the first need for timeouts, retries, idempotency and circuit breaking.

17Proposed Roadmap

Proposed, not approved. Sequencing reflects technical dependency and risk, not a commercial commitment.

Phase 1Pharmacy SaaS Largely delivered

POS, inventory, purchasing, branch management, subscription and the tenancy gate are built and tested.

  • Close the baseline defects: dead report surface, plaintext passwords, purchase-return credit rule
  • Fix the sales-discount defect before anything is built on promotions
  • Add storefront profile fields and geolocation — the prerequisite for everything after

Phase 2Customer marketplace Proposed

The first phase that needs genuinely new domains.

  • Consumer identity, addresses and app auth
  • Published catalogue read path (the one designed cross-tenant exception)
  • Location-based pharmacy discovery
  • Cart, order, acceptance and the state machine
  • Stock reservation
  • Push notification for both sides
  • Payment: start cash on delivery only, to defer the gateway

Phase 3Delivery Proposed

Deferrable — Phase 2 can launch pickup-only or pharmacy-delivered.

  • Delivery partner identity and app
  • Assignment
  • Pickup and proof of delivery
  • ETA and tracking
  • Failed delivery and return

Phase 4B2B commerce Proposed

The existing purchasing and settlement engine is the strongest asset here.

  • Supplier as a platform participant
  • Wholesale catalogue, MOQ and price
  • Purchase order preceding receipt
  • Credit terms

Phase 5Platform intelligence Proposed

Only worth building on real marketplace volume.

  • Platform GMV and commission reporting
  • Recommendation
  • Demand forecasting
  • Pharmacy benchmarking
