1System Overview

One installation of Friends Pharma ERP runs many independent pharmacies. Each may run several branches, and each branch trades on its own — its own stock, invoices, staff and permissions. The platform covers the whole trading cycle: buying from suppliers, holding stock by batch and expiry, selling at the counter, taking returns in both directions, delivering and collecting cash, and reporting on all of it.

The business problem it solves. A pharmacy's margin lives in details that are easy to lose — a supplier's free goods, a cashback at quarter end, a batch bought cheaply and sold after a price rise. Recorded loosely, none of it reaches the accounts. This system makes every one of them reduce the recorded cost of the goods that actually arrived, so profit reported is profit earned.

Architectural notes

ConcernHow it is handled
Money arithmeticFour stateless utility classes (PurchaseCalculator, SalesPriceCalculator, ProductPricingCalculator, TransactionCalculator). Pure functions, unit tested, so the create and update paths cannot drift apart.

How authorities are granted. A Super Admin (any ROLE01 holder) receives ROLE_ADMIN, ROLE_SUPER_ADMIN and ROLE_ROLE01..ROLE_ROLE09. Every other user receives exactly one authority: ROLE_<their code>. An annotation naming a role code no user is ever granted is therefore unreachable — and fails closed rather than loudly. That was the mechanism behind GAP-01.

Platform operator

A platform operator is isAdmin() and in the platform organisation. Both halves are required: the platform runs its own pharmacy, so being in that organisation alone does not make somebody an operator.

Two layers

Coarse access is the role annotation on the controller. Fine access is a per-branch matrix (POS_PERMISSIONS) read through PermissionGuard, gating View / Create / Edit / Delete / Approve / Export / Print per module.

One table holds four document types. 9Workflows

WF-01 — Purchase to Stock

Actors
Purchase Manager, Branch Administrator (approval)
Trigger
A supplier invoice arrives with the goods
Preconditions
Supplier exists and has a manufacturer assigned; products exist on the branch list
  1. Buyer selects the supplier. Their manufacturer decides which products may be added.
  2. Lines are entered — quantity, bonus, unit, TP%, discounts. A gift line carries quantity 0 and a bonus.
  3. The server ignores any client-supplied price and snapshots price from the product master.
  4. Line totals are computed (CALC-02 / CALC-03), then the invoice roll-up (CALC-05).
  5. Saved as Draft, submitted as Pending, or created directly Approved by a user holding Approve.
  6. On approval: header allocations reach the lines (CALC-06), gifts take their share (CALC-07), cost per piece is derived (CALC-08) and checked against MRP (CALC-09).
  7. A batch row and an inbound movement are written; the batch carries COST_PER_PCS.
  8. The invoice becomes payable and appears in V_PURCHASE_PAYABLE.
Failure
Validation refuses with a message naming the fix. Nothing is written; the transaction rolls back.
Postconditions
Stock on hand rises; supplier payable rises; the invoice is locked against editing.
flowchart TD
  A[Select supplier] --> B[Add lines]
  B --> C{Line shape valid?}
  C -->|No| C1[Refuse: name the fix]
  C -->|Yes| D{Products from this maker?}
  D -->|No, and not a gift| D1[Refuse: PRODUCT_NOT_FROM_SUPPLIER]
  D -->|Yes| E[Compute line totals and invoice]
  E --> F{Status}
  F -->|Draft| G[Editable]
  F -->|Pending| H[Await approval]
  F -->|Approved| I[Receive stock]
  H --> I
  I --> J{Cost per piece <= MRP?}
  J -->|No| J1[Refuse at approval]
  J -->|Yes| K[Write batch + movement]
  K --> L[Payable]

WF-02 — Sale to Margin

Actors
Pharmacist, Sales Manager
Trigger
A customer presents items at the counter
Preconditions
Stock on hand exists in at least one batch
  1. Items are added to a cart, by scan or search.
  2. Unit price derives from MRP and the sales percentage (CALC-10).
  3. On checkout, stock is issued FEFO — earliest expiry first.
  4. Each outbound movement copies the batch's COST_PER_PCS, fixing the cost of that sale.
  5. Revenue less that cost is gross profit (CALC-11).
Failure
Insufficient stock, or no permission to sell, refuses the checkout. The cart is preserved.
Postconditions
Stock falls; revenue and receivable rise; margin is fixed and cannot be changed by re-pricing.
flowchart TD
  A[Add to cart] --> B[Price from MRP and sales %]
  B --> C{Charge}
  C -->|No stock| C1[Refuse]
  C -->|OK| D[Issue FEFO across batches]
  D --> E[Copy batch cost onto each movement]
  E --> F[Invoice raised]
  F --> G[Margin = revenue - snapshot cost]

WF-03 — Supplier Settlement

Actors
Accounts
Trigger
A payment is due or a supplier is paid
Preconditions
An approved purchase exists
  1. Payment is recorded against one or more invoices, or held as an unallocated advance.
  2. V_SUPPLIER_PAYMENT_LINE splits it into INVOICE and MANUAL sources.
  3. V_PURCHASE_PAYABLE nets billed against paid and return credit (CALC-13).
  4. V_SUPPLIER_ACCOUNT_BRANCH aggregates per supplier per branch.
Failure
Overpayment beyond tolerance is refused or held as an advance.
Postconditions
Outstanding falls; advances are carried until recovered.
flowchart LR
  A[Payment] --> B{Allocated?}
  B -->|To invoices| C[APPLIED against V_PURCHASE_PAYABLE]
  B -->|Unallocated| D[ADVANCE_AMOUNT held]
  C --> E[Outstanding falls]
  D --> F[Recovered later]

WF-04 — Inter-Branch Requisition

Actors
Requesting branch, approving branch
Trigger
A branch is short of stock another holds
Preconditions
Both branches belong to the same organisation
  1. A requisition is raised in DRAFT and lines are added.
  2. It is SUBMITTED for approval.
  3. A user holding Approve on Requisition approves or rejects it.
  4. An approved requisition is CONVERTED into the movement that fulfils it.
Failure
Rejection sets REJECTED with a reason; the stock never moves.
Postconditions
CONVERTED requisitions are closed and cannot be edited.
flowchart TD
  A[DRAFT] --> B[SUBMITTED]
  B --> C{Approve right?}
  C -->|No| C1[403 APPROVE_NOT_PERMITTED]
  C -->|Reject| D[REJECTED]
  C -->|Approve| E[APPROVED]
  E --> F[CONVERTED]
  A --> G[CANCELLED]

WF-05 — Password Reset

Actors
Platform operator, account holder
Trigger
A user cannot sign in
Preconditions
The caller is ROLE01 and in the platform organisation
  1. The operator finds the account. Credential state is shown; the password is not, and cannot be.
  2. Reset is confirmed against the account's name, role and branch.
  3. A 16-character temporary password is generated (CALC-15), hashed, and shown exactly once.

Failure

Postconditions

Every formula below is quoted from the class that computes it. Where a live row existed the worked example was cross-checked against it.

Trace: frontend to database

C-01Product derived pricing

Source · ProductPricingCalculator

TP_PRICE  = ROUND(MRP − (MRP × CONVERSION_RATE / 100), 2)
IGTA      = ROUND(TP_PRICE × VAT_PCT / 100, 2)
BUY_PRICE = ROUND(TP_PRICE + (TP_PRICE × VAT_PCT / 100), 2)

CONVERSION_RATE defaults to 25.037; VAT_PCT defaults to 17.40. Nulls are treated as zero (NVL).

Worked example

Given MRP = 250.00, CONVERSION_RATE = 25.037, VAT_PCT = 17.40

TP_PRICE  = 250.00 − (250.00 × 25.037 / 100) = 250.00 − 62.5925 = 187.4075 → 187.41
IGTA      = 187.41 × 17.40 / 100 = 32.609... → 32.61
BUY_PRICE = 187.41 + 32.61 = 220.02

Result TP 187.41 · IGTA 32.61 · Buy 220.02 — matching product 3 (Napa 500mg) in the live master.

C-02Purchase line total — MNP basis

Source · PurchaseCalculator.calculateLineTotal

base      = ROUND(qty × (tp + igta) − (qty × tp × tpPct / 100), 2)
afterDisc = ROUND(base − base × discountPct / 100, 2)
total     = afterDisc − (qty × flatDiscount)

The flat discount is applied AFTER the final rounding on this branch, and is multiplied by quantity. This asymmetry with the MRP branch is deliberate and is a faithful port of the legacy APEX expression; totals must keep matching the previous system.

Worked example

Given qty = 10, tp = 187.41, igta = 32.61, tpPct = 3, discountPct = 5, flat = 2

gross     = 10 × (187.41 + 32.61) = 2,200.20
tpCut     = 10 × 187.41 × 3 / 100 = 56.223
base      = ROUND(2,200.20 − 56.223, 2) = 2,143.98
afterDisc = ROUND(2,143.98 − 107.199, 2) = 2,036.78
total     = 2,036.78 − (10 × 2) = 2,016.78

Result Line total 2,016.78

C-03Purchase line total — MRP basis

Source · PurchaseCalculator.calculateLineTotal

total = ROUND(qty × (mrp − (mrp × discountPct / 100 + flatDiscount)), 2)

Here the flat discount is per unit and is applied BEFORE rounding. TP% is not used at all on this basis.

Worked example

Given qty = 100, mrp = 250.00, discountPct = 0, flat = 0

perUnit = 250.00 − (0 + 0) = 250.00
total = ROUND(100 × 250.00, 2) = 25,000.00

Result Line total 25,000.00

C-04Line gross (for TOTAL_DISCOUNT)

Source · PurchaseCalculator.calculateLineGross

gross = calculateLineTotal(type, qty, tp, igta, mrp, tpPct = 0, discountPct = 0, flatDiscount = 0)

tpPct is zeroed along with the two discounts. Previously it was passed through, so the trade-price cut was treated as part of list price and never appeared in TOTAL_DISCOUNT — on invoice PU260900007 a 3% TP cut worth 5.62 vanished and the invoice reported 21.46 given away where the supplier had actually given 27.08.

Worked example

Given The C-02 line

gross = 10 × (187.41 + 32.61) = 2,200.20
line discount = 2,200.20 − 2,016.78 = 183.42

Result 183.42 reported as given away on that line (56.22 TP + 107.20 pct + 20.00 flat)

C-05Purchase invoice roll-up

Source · PurchaseCalculator.calculateInvoice

total         = ROUND(Σ line.TOTAL_PRICE, 2)
invoiceDisAmt = ROUND(total × invoiceDisPct / 100, 2)
payable       = ROUND(total − invoiceDisAmt + tax + serviceCharge − cashback, 2)
totalDiscount = ROUND(MAX(Σ gross − total, 0) + invoiceDisAmt, 2)
dueAmount     = ROUND(payable − givenAmount, 2)

Cashback is subtracted AFTER tax and service charge: it is money handed back on the whole bill, not a reduction in the price of the goods before tax was computed. It is kept separate from invoiceDisAmt because a discount is a price and a cashback is an incentive — different events with different lives. A negative dueAmount means the supplier was overpaid.

Worked example

Given lines total 2,283.33, invoiceDisPct 0, tax 0, service 0, cashback 1,000, given 0

invoiceDisAmt = 0.00
payable = 2,283.33 − 0 + 0 + 0 − 1,000.00 = 1,283.33
dueAmount = 1,283.33 − 0 = 1,283.33

Result AMOUNT_TOBE_PAID 1,283.33 — matching live invoice PU260900027.

C-06Net line value after header allocations

Source · StockPostingServiceImpl.netLineTotal

net = line.TOTAL_PRICE
if invoiceDisPct > 0:  net −= ROUND(net × invoiceDisPct / 100, 2)
if cashback > 0 and invoiceTotal > 0:
     weight = line.TOTAL_PRICE / invoiceTotal   (scale 10)
     net   −= ROUND(cashback × weight, 2)
net = MAX(net, 0)

A header-level reduction must reach the goods or the shelf is valued at a price nobody paid. The discount is a percentage so each line's share falls straight out of it; cashback is an amount, so the share is the line's weight in the invoice. The zero-total guard prevents a division by zero turning an incentive into an exception on approval. The floor at zero prevents a cashback that swallowed a line from putting negative cost into a batch.

Worked example

Given Line 2,083.33 on invoice PU260900027 (total 2,283.33, cashback 1,000)

weight = 2,083.33 / 2,283.33 = 0.9124...
share  = ROUND(1,000 × 0.9124, 2) = 912.41
net    = 2,083.33 − 912.41 = 1,170.92

Result 1,170.92 before gift allocation

C-07Gift cost allocation

Source · StockPostingServiceImpl.CostAllocation

if no line has LINE_KIND = 'GIFT':  netFor(line) = netLineTotal(line)   (identity)
otherwise:
     invoiceNet    = Σ netLineTotal(line)
     sellingValue  = line.MRP_PRICE × (line.QUANTITY + line.BONUS_QTY)
     netFor(line)  = invoiceNet × sellingValue / Σ sellingValue

A gift line has no money of its own. Left at zero it would report an infinite margin, so the invoice's cost is spread across every line by what each is worth at retail. Invoices without a gift keep the identity allocation and are unchanged.

Worked example

Given PU260900027: bought 100 Napa (MRP 20.8333/pc) + 10 bonus; 50 Ace (MRP 4.00); gift 20 Sergel (MRP 12.3333). Cashback 1,000.

invoiceNet = 2,283.33 − 1,000 = 1,283.33
sellingValue Napa  = 20.8333 × 110 = 2,291.66
sellingValue Ace   = 4.0000 × 50  =   200.00
sellingValue Sergel= 12.3333 × 20 =   246.67
Σ sellingValue = 2,738.33
Napa net   = 1,283.33 × 2,291.66 / 2,738.33 = 1,073.99
Sergel net = 1,283.33 ×   246.67 / 2,738.33 =   115.60

Result The gift carries 115.60 of real cost rather than zero

C-08Received pieces and cost per piece

Source · StockPostingServiceImpl.receivedPcs / unitCost

receivedPcs  = (QUANTITY + BONUS_QTY) × COALESCE(NO_OF_PCS, 1)
COST_PER_PCS = netFor(line) / receivedPcs

The bonus is in the divisor, not the numerator. That is the whole mechanism by which free goods lower the cost of a batch: the same money is spread over more pieces. receivedPcs is deliberately kept separate from the shared toPcs() helper, which two outbound call sites use and which must NOT include the bonus.

Worked example

Given Napa line from C-07: net 1,073.99, bought 100 + 10 bonus, NO_OF_PCS = 1 (PCS basis)

receivedPcs = (100 + 10) × 1 = 110
COST_PER_PCS = 1,073.99 / 110 = 9.763545...

Result 9.763636 stored on the batch — verified against the live POS_STOCK_BATCH row

C-09Cost guard on receipt

Source · StockPostingServiceImpl.requireCostBelowMrp

refuse when COST_PER_PCS > MRP per piece

A batch costing more than it can be sold for is a data-entry error caught at approval rather than a loss discovered at the till. The message states the multiple.

Worked example

Given cost 32.00/pc against MRP 20.83/pc

32.00 / 20.83 = 1.5

Result Refused: cost is 1.5× the MRP

C-10Sales price from MRP

Source · SalesPriceCalculator

Unit Sales Price = ROUND(MRP − (MRP × SalesPct / 100), 2)
Discount Amount  = MRP − Unit Sales Price
percentForPrice  = (MRP − target) / MRP × 100
Worked example

Given MRP 250.00, SalesPct 8

250.00 × 8 / 100 = 20.00
250.00 − 20.00 = 230.00

Result Sells at 230.00, discount 20.00

C-11COGS and gross profit

Source · DashboardAnalyticsRepository / AiAnalyticsRepository

COGS         = Σ (movement.QTY_PCS × COALESCE(movement.COST_PER_PCS, 0))
Revenue      = Σ sale line value
Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS
Margin %     = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100

COGS reads the movement's own COST_PER_PCS — the cost of the specific batch that went out, snapshotted at the moment of sale — not the product's current cost. Re-pricing a product therefore cannot retrospectively change last month's profit.

Worked example

Given 60 pieces issued from the C-08 batch at 9.7636, sold for 20.00 each

COGS = 60 × 9.7636 = 585.82
Revenue = 60 × 20.00 = 1,200.00
Profit = 614.18

Result Margin 51.2%

C-12Stock valuation

Source · DashboardAnalyticsRepository.stockValue

Stock Value = Σ (batch.QTY_PCS × COALESCE(batch.COST_PER_PCS, 0))

Valued at cost, never at MRP, and per remaining batch quantity — so an aged batch bought cheaply is not revalued upward by a later price rise.

Worked example

Given 110 pcs at 9.7636 and 50 pcs at 1.8746

110 × 9.7636 = 1,074.00
50 × 1.8746 = 93.73

Result 1,167.73

C-13Supplier payable and ageing

Source · V_PURCHASE_PAYABLE

BILLED   = COALESCE(AMOUNT_TOBE_PAID, 0)
DUE      = BILLED − PAID_AT_INVOICE − PAID_LATER − RETURN_CREDIT
AGE_DAYS = CURRENT_DATE − TRANSACTION_DATE

PAID_AT_INVOICE is payment lines with SOURCE='INVOICE'; PAID_LATER is SOURCE='MANUAL'. RETURN_CREDIT counts only approved purchase returns whose PAYMENT_TYPE is 'ADJUSTMENT' — see Gap G-05. Outstanding is deliberately NOT date-filtered: it is a position as at today, and narrowing it to a period would drop older unpaid bills and report a smaller debt than exists.

Worked example

Given Billed 1,283.33; paid 500 at invoice; return credit 0

DUE = 1,283.33 − 500 − 0 − 0 = 783.33

Result 783.33 outstanding

C-14Supplier benefit reporting

Source · SalesPurchaseReportRepository.supplierBenefit / ReportGridServiceImpl

bonusPcs  = Σ (BONUS_QTY × COALESCE(NO_OF_PCS,1))  where LINE_KIND = 'PURCHASE'
giftPcs   = Σ (BONUS_QTY × COALESCE(NO_OF_PCS,1))  where LINE_KIND = 'GIFT'
giftValue = Σ (BONUS_QTY × MRP_PRICE)              where LINE_KIND = 'GIFT'
benefit   = cashback + giftValue
benefit % = benefit / (billed + cashback) × 100

Quantities are converted to pieces because BONUS_QTY is held in each line's own sales unit — adding 10 boxes to 10 strips gives 20 of nothing. Money is NOT converted, because MRP_PRICE is also stored per sales unit and already matches. The bonus is never valued: it is already banked as a lower cost per piece (C-08), and pricing it again would count one benefit twice. The ratio divides by the gross bill because AMOUNT_TOBE_PAID has already had the cashback removed.

Worked example

Given cashback 1,000; gift 20 boxes of 12 at MRP 148; billed 46,283.33

giftPcs = 20 × 12 = 240
giftValue = 20 × 148 = 2,960.00
benefit = 3,960.00
gross = 47,283.33

Result 8.4% benefit

C-15Temporary password strength

Source · TemporaryPassword

16 characters from a 25-symbol alphabet ≈ log2(25^16) ≈ 74.3 bits

The alphabet excludes characters that are ambiguous when read aloud (O/0, I/l/1, S/5, B/8, Z/2) because the password is delivered by voice; length pays back the lost entropy. Uses SecureRandom.nextInt(bound), not modulo, which would bias toward the low end of the alphabet.

Worked example

Given —

25^16 ≈ 2.33 × 10^22

Result ≈ 74 bits, formatted ABCD-EFGH-JKMN-PQRT

Two decisions only the business can make

  1. Which purchase returns reduce what a supplier is owed. Today almost none do, so supplier balances are overstated wherever goods went back.
  2. Whether a sales discount rule belongs to a branch or to the whole pharmacy. The feature has never worked because the two answers were never reconciled.
